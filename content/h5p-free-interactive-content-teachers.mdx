**H5P gives teachers more than 50 free interactive activities, and the whole thing is open source on GitHub.** Interactive videos, drag-and-drop quizzes, branching scenarios, flashcards, timelines — all of it free, all of it MIT licensed, and none of it needs you to write a line of code.

If you have ever wished a worksheet could ask a question back, this is the tool that does it.

## What H5P actually is

H5P, short for HTML5 Package, is a free and open-source framework for creating interactive HTML5 content. The University of Saskatchewan's teaching guide puts it plainly: it is a collection of tools for creating interactive content, with **over 50 content types** that support engaging and meaningful learning experiences.

The list reads like a teacher's wish list. **Interactive Video** lets you pause a video and drop a question right at the moment that matters. **Course Presentation** turns slides into something students click through and answer. **Branching Scenario** builds choose-your-own-adventure lessons where a student's choice changes what happens next. Then there is **Drag and Drop**, **Drag the Words**, **Fill in the Blanks**, **Multiple Choice**, **Question Set**, **Flashcards**, **Memory Game**, **Image Hotspots**, **Timeline**, **Audio Recorder**, and dozens more.

It does not live in its own app that students have to download. It plugs into the platforms schools already use: **Moodle, WordPress, Drupal and Canvas**, with native integrations. I verified the WordPress plugin against its real repository today: it is the **h5p/h5p-wordpress-plugin** repo on GitHub, **MIT licensed, version 1.17.10**, and actively maintained, with its last update in October 2026. The Interactive Video content type's own repo was updated in November 2026, so this is not an abandoned project.

And the activities are reusable by design. H5P content can be exported, imported, embedded and moved between compatible platforms. The eCampus Ontario H5P Studio, a central library where educators share their work, holds **more than 1,000 activities**, most of them Creative Commons licensed, ready to download and adapt. That means you rarely start from a blank page.

## Why it matters: interactivity is not a luxury feature

Most teachers hear "interactive content" and picture one of two things: an expensive app with a monthly subscription, or a video editor they do not have time to learn. So the default stays the same — a slide deck, a printed worksheet, a video the class watches in silence.

That is the mistake H5P quietly fixes. The research on active learning has been consistent for years: students remember more when they have to do something with the material, not just look at it. A video that stops and asks "which wire goes where, and why?" at the exact second the wiring appears on screen is a different lesson from the same video played straight through. The first one makes every student commit to an answer. The second one lets half the room drift.

H5P matters because it removes the two excuses that usually kill this idea: cost and skill. It costs nothing, and building your first activity takes about the same effort as writing a quiz in a document. The barrier was never the pedagogy. It was the tooling. H5P takes the tooling off the table.

## In my classroom

I teach ICT and robotics in Bangkok, and my Year 7 and 8 students spend a lot of time watching short build videos — how a micro:bit circuit is wired, how a robot arm moves, how a loop runs in Python. The videos were good. The watching was passive. Eyes on the screen, brains somewhere else.

So I rebuilt one of those lessons as an H5P Interactive Video. Same video, but now it pauses three times: once to ask which pin the sensor connects to, once to ask what the code will do next, and once at the end to ask students to predict the result before the video shows it. A wrong answer does not punish them, it just shows a short explanation and lets them try again.

The change was immediate and visible. Students who used to zone out during videos now lean in, because the video will not move on until they answer. And I get something a plain video never gave me: I can see which question the class got wrong, which tells me exactly what to reteach the next day.

I also use **Drag the Words** for Python keywords with my younger classes. Take a short program, blank out the keywords, and students drag `for`, `if` and `print` into place. It takes five minutes to build and it turns a vocabulary drill into a puzzle. My students argue about where `while` goes, which is exactly the argument I want them to have.

## Steal this: five steps to your first H5P activity tomorrow

You can do this in one free period, and you do not need your school's IT department for any of it:

1. **Play with the examples first.** Go to h5p.org and open the examples. Click through an Interactive Video and a Drag and Drop. Pick the one that makes you think "my students would like this."
2. **Start with one content type, not fifty.** Fifty options is overwhelming, so ignore forty-nine of them. Interactive Video and Drag the Words are the two best first builds for most teachers.
3. **Use the free account or your own platform.** Make a free account at h5p.org to build and share your first activities. If your school runs WordPress or Moodle, install the free H5P plugin instead, and your activities live inside the platform you already use.
4. **Steal before you build.** Search the eCampus Ontario H5P Studio or the examples on h5p.org for your topic. Download an activity that is close, change the questions to match your lesson, and you have a finished activity in ten minutes.
5. **Put it where students already go.** Embed the activity in your lesson page, your class site or your LMS. One interactive activity inside a normal lesson beats a whole library of activities nobody opens.

## The honest caveat

H5P is not a learning management system, and it does not pretend to be one. On its own it has no gradebook and no class roster, so if you need scores recorded, you need it inside Moodle, WordPress or another platform with the plugin. The free account at h5p.org also has limits, and there is a paid hosted service called H5P.com — do not confuse the two. The technology is free and open source; the paid service is just one company hosting it for you. And the fifty-plus content types are a strength that can become a trap: if you try to learn them all at once, you will build nothing. Pick one, ship it, then pick the next.
